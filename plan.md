# Live battle improvements plan

## Target and current state

Target the live match at `/battle/play/[id]` (the `/battle/play/` route family), with the lobby and Convex changes required to make the new flow real. Today `app/battle/lobby/[id]/page.tsx` and `convex/lobby.ts` select **one NFT per player**; `convex/schema.ts` and `convex/battle.ts` store and fight with that single NFT. `app/battle/play/[id]/page.tsx` instead renders move names as separate cards in the hand. The current `components/battle/tcg/BattleCard.tsx` wraps the NFT image inside a generic Pokémon-style card frame. The existing `plan.md` assumed one-card battles; this plan supersedes that assumption.

Use `tcg/src/components/battle/`, `tcg/src/battle/animation/AnimationQueue.ts`, and `tcg/src/app/globals.css` as animation/interaction references, **not** as sources of Pokémon assets, scripted matches, rules, or branding. The reference card flip is tied to a prize-card reveal; adapt the visual effect to a real roster reveal instead of adding fake prizes. Keep the live match driven by Convex, not the reference's mock sequence.

## Combat rules chosen for implementation

- Each player locks 3–5 NFTs in the lobby. After both rosters reveal, each chooses exactly three and selects one starting active card. The higher-speed starting card takes the first turn; a tie favors the lobby creator.
- The other two chosen cards are reserves; the remaining roster cards cannot be played. Each usable card has its own HP. A voluntary switch uses the player's turn. After a KO, the owner must pick a surviving reserve before taking their turn; this forced replacement does not consume another turn.
- Only the active card's moves may attack. Damage continues to use server-side stats and critical/type calculation, not a UI power number. A player wins when all three opponent lineup cards reach zero HP. Existing one-card records retain their original rules.

**Security follow-up before production:** the existing Convex battle/lobby APIs accept a client-supplied wallet address, and NFT sync accepts client-supplied ownership data. Checking the synced owner/user record is useful for roster validation but does **not** authenticate the caller or prove current on-chain ownership. Add a server-verified wallet signature/challenge (with replay protection) and verify Metaplex Core asset ownership against trusted chain data before treating battle roster validation or winner rewards as secure. This is not solved by a connected browser wallet alone.

## Ordered tasks

1. **Specify and persist the 3–5 card roster and three-card lineup.** In the lobby, let each participant select **3 to 5 distinct owned NFTs** before readying up; show a count and prevent ready/start with fewer than three or more than five. Store each player's selected roster in `convex/schema.ts` and `convex/lobby.ts`, validate membership, ownership and synced stats server-side, and reset readiness when the roster changes. After both rosters are locked, reveal the opponent's roster (images and statistics) to each player; let each choose and confirm **exactly three** of their own roster cards for use in the battle. Persist both lineups before combat becomes active, so reloads, two clients, and races cannot bypass or change the selection. The unchosen 0–2 cards remain visible for inspection but are unusable. Provide an explicit state while waiting for the opponent to confirm. Keep existing battle records readable via optional/versioned fields and a legacy one-card rendering path; do not retroactively turn old matches into three-card games.

2. **Define authoritative three-card combat before changing the UI.** Extend battle state for each card's identity, current/max HP, active card, reserve/KO state, and match outcome. Decide and document the exact initial active-card choice, switch timing/turn cost, KO replacement, and victory condition before implementation; a straightforward proposed rule is one active card plus two reserves, switching consumes a turn, a KO requires selecting a surviving replacement, and victory occurs when all three usable cards are knocked out. Validate card identity, lineup membership, active status, move name and turn in `convex/battle.ts`; derive damage from the active card's stats and retain winner rewards without double payout. Fetch metadata/moves for all relevant cards in `getBattleWithNFTData`. Update `/battle` active/history summaries and lobby displays that currently assume `player1NFT`/`player2NFT` are the entire team. Do not present a move as having a different power unless server rules support it.

3. **Replace move cards with a move hover box.** Remove the `moveCards` mapping and move-card hand from `app/battle/play/[id]/page.tsx`. Put the player's three chosen NFT images in the current bottom-hand position. Offer the **active card's** real custom moves through a compact hover/focus box near that card (and a tap/click-open alternative on touch devices); show names, descriptions, selection and disabled states. Keep the Play Move confirmation button and call the validated `executeTurn` mutation. Inspection and move selection must be separate actions; hovering or inspecting must never submit a turn.

4. **Use the actual NFT image as the card.** Refactor the live battle rendering in `components/battle/tcg/BattleCard.tsx` (or create a live-only NFT image component) so the board and three-card row display the full NFT artwork itself, not an illustration embedded in a template with “POKÉMON,” fake numbers, footer labels, or an HP bar. Keep HP and turn/status information in the existing separate rails or compact labels *outside* the artwork. Resolve images with `getNFTMetadata`/`getIpfsImageUrl`; use a neutral fallback only when the NFT art is missing or fails to load. Remove leftover Pokémon branding from the live route, including the top bar and accessibility text.

5. **Open an inspect window on the left.** Selecting an owned, active, reserve, or revealed opponent card opens a small dismissible panel on the left showing a larger, uncropped NFT image, name, type, current/max HP (where applicable), attack, defense, speed, strength, intelligence, luck, and available move details/description. Use real metadata/stats; do not show mock power values. Make it keyboard-operable, give it a close control and sensible focus behavior, and reflow it on narrow screens without hiding the battle actions. Inspecting a card does not change the active card or the selected move.

6. **Migrate applicable `/tcg` animations into live battles.** Inventory `tcg/src/components/battle/{BattleClient,VfxLayer,SceneEffects,PrizeOverlay}.tsx`, `tcg/src/battle/animation/AnimationQueue.ts`, and the corresponding animation CSS. Adapt card reveal/flip for the locked-roster reveal, card travel/placement for selecting and switching actual NFT cards, focus/hover and inspect-panel transitions, attack focus/projectile/impact, real damage and critical-hit feedback, KO/replacement, turn indicator and result transitions. Drive every effect from confirmed Convex state/move IDs (or a confirmed mutation result), with correct attacker/target/card, real damage values, replay/refresh deduplication, cleanup/cancellation and a skip/reduced-motion path. Reuse scoped CSS or existing motion dependencies where possible; do not import the whole demo stylesheet or require GSAP solely for the reference queue. Exclude prize, energy, trainer, stadium and scripted character animations unless corresponding mechanics actually exist; do not display hard-coded `250` damage or fake events.

7. **Verify the flow and presentation.** Exercise two wallets from lobby creation through roster lock, opponent analysis, three-card confirmation, attacks, switching, KO/replacement and result; cover 3/4/5-card rosters, insufficient owned NFTs, duplicate/invalid selections, forged move/card submissions, double-confirm/double-turn, disconnect/reload, missing images/metadata, and old one-card battles. Check desktop/mobile, keyboard/touch and reduced-motion behavior. Run `tsc --noEmit`, a non-writing Biome check on changed files and the production build; fix any failures introduced by the changes.

8. **Make multiple focused commits so the work is easy to track and push to the remote.** Commit the backend/schema and compatibility work, roster/lineup UI, live card/move/inspect UI, animation migration, and validation fixes separately with descriptive messages. Review the diff and status before each commit; do not include unrelated or secret files. Push to the intended remote only when authorized and the branch/remote are confirmed.

## Done when

- Each new match starts from two locked 3–5 NFT rosters, and each player can choose exactly three usable cards after seeing the opponent's roster.
- The three selected NFT artworks replace move cards in the live hand; attacks are chosen from a hover/focus/touch-accessible move box, and every card is inspectable in a left-side stats panel.
- Live effects reflect actual server events and honor reduced motion; existing matches and rewards still work, and the route contains no Pokémon placeholder frame or branding.
